## The person who doesn't just read from the script

Every customer service team has queries that don't fit the standard process — the complicated complaint, the technical question, the customer who's already been passed around three times. A customer service specialist is trained to be the person who actually resolves it, rather than escalating it further.

Across the apprenticeship, learners build real skills in handling complex and technical customer issues, gathering and acting on customer feedback, and becoming a genuine advocate for service quality — across contact centres, retail, webchat or any customer-facing environment. It's a strong next step for a customer service advisor, administrator, consultant or representative who is ready for more, with progression toward team leader, supervisor and management-level apprenticeships beyond.

## What's involved before you enroll

### **A good fit for**

Anyone already working in a customer-facing role — contact centres, retail, webchat or service teams — who's ready to take on the complex cases and become a genuine point of escalation. That includes customer service advisors, administrators, consultants and representatives looking for a recognised step up.

### **Before you apply**

Applicants need to be in a relevant customer service role, with a willingness to build on existing skills. GCSEs (Grade 4/C or equivalent) in English and maths are typically required, or completed alongside the apprenticeship.

### **Life after Level 3**

A strong foundation for team leader, supervisor or customer service manager roles, and a genuine stepping stone toward Level 4 and 5 apprenticeships in leadership and management.

### **Who covers it**

Delivered as a government-funded apprenticeship through the Growth & Skills Levy, with levy and non-levy options. Our team confirms exactly what your business would pay before you commit — see our funding pages for the full detail, or the “What does it cost?” question below for the short answer.

## What the final assessment involves

The end-point assessment (EPA) sits at the close of the apprenticeship and checks the apprentice against the knowledge, skills and behaviours in the occupational standard. It's the moment to show an independent assessor they can do the job they've trained for.

An independent end-point assessment organisation (EPAO) delivers it, typically combining a practical observation with questioning and a work-based project supported by a professional discussion. WTD and the employer make sure the apprentice knows exactly what to expect and how to prepare. On this programme the EPA period follows the 15 months of training.

## Questions people ask before applying

[Request a skills assessment](https://wtd.org.uk/contact/)

How does an apprenticeship like this actually work?

A structured programme that pairs a real, paid customer-facing job with formal training toward a nationally recognised qualification — here, the Level 3 Customer Service Specialist standard. Off-the-job training takes place during the apprentice's normal working hours; the minimum amount required is set by the apprenticeship standard and agreed with the apprentice and employer as part of the training plan.

What does a customer service specialist do differently from an advisor?

A customer service advisor typically follows established processes for everyday queries. A customer service specialist is trained to go further — handling the complex, technical or escalated cases that don't fit a standard script, and acting as a genuine point of expertise within the team.

Is this a customer service job board or vacancy listing?

No — WTD doesn't list job vacancies directly, and this page isn't a substitute for a jobs board. This is funded training that runs alongside a real, paid customer service role with an employer. If you're an employer with customer service staff — advisors, administrators, consultants or representatives — this is how you train them into a specialist role. If you're looking to start a customer service career, you'll typically need a relevant role in place first, whether with your current employer or a new one; delivery itself is online and available UK-wide, including for roles based in London.

Do I need to already be working in customer service to start?

Yes, generally. This standard builds on existing customer service experience rather than starting from zero — it's designed for people already in a customer-facing role who are ready to take on more complex cases.

Does this lead to a customer service manager role?

It can be a strong step toward one, but it isn't a management qualification in itself. This Level 3 standard builds deep customer service expertise; team leader, supervisor and management responsibilities usually follow as a next step, sometimes via a further Level 4 or 5 leadership apprenticeship.
